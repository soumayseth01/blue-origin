# Sponsor-Backed NewCo and Founding CEO Framework

**Business structure, economics, governance, and operating model**  
**Status:** Negotiation draft v0.9  
**Date:** July 27, 2026  
**Currency:** U.S. dollars

> This is a commercial planning document, not legal, tax, accounting, employment, or investment advice. The final structure must be implemented by qualified counsel and advisers in the relevant jurisdictions.

**Non-binding status.** This document is a discussion draft only. Except for a confidentiality, exclusivity, expense, or similar provision expressly identified as binding in a separately signed writing, it does not create any obligation to form, fund, join, employ, issue equity, purchase services, source revenue, or enter a transaction. Obligations arise only under executed definitive agreements.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Create a genuinely independent operating company ("NewCo") sponsored by the Managing Partners through a single sponsor holding entity. NewCo should not be operated as a division, cost center, or informal extension of Agent Ramp, HSG, or another consulting business.

The recommended opening package is:

| Item | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Legal form | U.S. private corporation; Delaware C-corporation assumed for planning, subject to tax and legal review |
| Sponsor / Managing Partner ownership | 60% fully diluted at formation |
| Founding CEO ownership | 25% fully diluted at formation, outside the employee pool |
| Employee equity pool | 15% fully diluted and reserved at formation |
| CEO vesting | 36 months, monthly, with a six-month cliff; credit from the date substantive formation work begins |
| CEO base salary | $200,000 per year |
| CEO target bonus | $100,000, or 50% of base salary |
| CEO maximum bonus | $200,000, or 100% of base salary |
| Sponsor capital commitment | $4.0M, legally committed at closing; intended to cover the full Base Plan plus contingency and working capital |
| Year 1 net-revenue target | $5.0M |
| Year 1 sponsor-channel commitment | At least $3.0M of eligible recognized NewCo net revenue **and** $1.35M of NewCo gross profit, with at least 95% of due invoices collected |
| Year 1 gross-margin target | 50% of net revenue; normal deal floor of 45% |
| Governance | Five-member board: three Sponsor nominees, the Founding CEO, and one mutually selected independent director |
| Operating authority | CEO has full day-to-day authority within the board-approved plan, budget, risk limits, and delegation matrix |
| Affiliate relationship | Written commercial-partner and intercompany-services agreements; no informal revenue sharing, staff sharing, or expense allocation |

The proposed $4.0M capital commitment and agreed Year 1 commercial support form the commercial basis for the Sponsor's 60% ownership. Funding the approved commitment must not create further dilution for the CEO or the employee pool.

## 2. Planning assumptions and terms to confirm

This draft makes the following assumptions so that the parties have a complete proposal to negotiate:

1. NewCo will be formed in the United States and will initially be remote-first.
2. The CEO will be a U.S. employee. The CEO's work state remains to be confirmed.
3. The Sponsor ownership will be held through one entity rather than directly by several Managing Partners. The beneficial ownership split within that entity is a Sponsor matter.
4. Agent Ramp and HSG are separate affiliate businesses that can introduce, prime, subcontract, refer, or purchase work from NewCo. Their exact legal relationship remains to be confirmed.
5. No Agent Ramp or HSG customer contract, employee, asset, sales vehicle, or revenue automatically belongs to NewCo. Any transfer requires a separate written agreement and any required customer or third-party consent.
6. The Base Plan includes a U.S. leadership and commercial team, a solutions and delivery organization, business-capability leadership, and an offshore delivery hub. Detailed product matters are outside this document.
7. Public-sector sales may be relevant, but the initial sales model will also support commercial enterprise customers.
8. The financial plan is a gross funding commitment. Forecast revenue extends runway; uncontracted pipeline does not replace committed capital.

The parties should confirm these assumptions before definitive documents are signed.

## 3. Legal entity and capitalization

### 3.1 Fully diluted opening cap table

The cap table should be fixed after giving effect to the entire 15% employee pool and the Sponsor's complete formation and first-year funding commitment.

| Holder | Fully diluted percentage | Illustrative shares on a 10M-share basis |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sponsor holding company | 60% | 6,000,000 |
| Founding CEO | 25% | 2,500,000 |
| Employee equity pool, reserved and ungranted | 15% | 1,500,000 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **10,000,000** |

"Fully diluted" should include issued shares, the entire reserved pool, options, warrants, SAFEs, convertible instruments, and every other right to acquire equity.

If only the Sponsor and CEO shares are issued at closing, their percentages of then-outstanding voting stock will differ from their fully diluted percentages. Definitive documents must distinguish clearly among authorized, issued, outstanding, and fully diluted equity.

### 3.2 Sponsor ownership and funding obligation

The Sponsor's 60% should be issued against:

- A binding $4.0M funding commitment.
- Formation support and access to Managing Partner relationships.
- The commercial commitments in Section 8.
- Any separately identified and independently valued business assets contributed at closing.

If the $4.0M cash commitment were the only consideration for 60%, it would imply a $6.67M post-money value and a $2.67M pre-money value. Because this proposal also requires commercial access and revenue support, the parties should record how those obligations influence the agreed value and avoid counting the same contribution again through automatic affiliate fees.

The security class is a required closing decision. The CEO's opening position should be one class of voting common stock for Sponsor and CEO. If the Sponsor requires preferred stock, its economics must be modeled separately and should ordinarily be limited to a 1x non-participating liquidation preference, with no cumulative dividend, mandatory redemption, or participating return unless expressly priced into the deal. A 60% as-converted percentage does not reveal the economic effect of a liquidation preference.

Recommended issuance sequence:

1. Incorporate NewCo and approve the initial capitalization.
2. Obtain a supportable formation-date fair-market-value analysis for the common stock.
3. Issue the CEO's restricted common stock for documented consideration before or contemporaneously with the Sponsor financing, using tax and securities advice.
4. Execute the Sponsor subscription and funding agreement and fund the closing tranche.
5. Release Sponsor shares from escrow in direct proportion to funded cash: 4.5M of the illustrative 6.0M shares after $3.0M is funded and the remaining 1.5M shares after the final $1.0M is funded.
6. Adopt the employee pool and complete all board, stockholder, tax, and securities records.

An 83(b) election changes the timing of taxation; it does not establish or reduce fair market value. The CEO should not accept a nominal-price grant after the Sponsor investment has created a supportable higher common-stock value without specific tax advice.

Recommended protections:

- Put the funding obligation in a Sponsor Subscription and Funding Agreement, not in a board presentation or non-binding side letter.
- Either deposit the entire commitment at closing or make later tranches automatic and enforceable.
- Hold a corresponding portion of Sponsor shares in escrow or subject them to company repurchase if a committed tranche is not funded after notice and cure.
- Treat funding within the approved $4.0M commitment as non-dilutive.
- Treat capital beyond the commitment as a board-approved shareholder loan or a financing in which all eligible holders receive the agreed participation rights.
- Do not issue extra Sponsor equity merely because sales take longer than forecast.

### 3.3 Founding CEO equity

Recommended commercial terms:

- Grant 25% at formation as restricted common stock subject to reverse vesting, rather than as an undocumented promise of a future grant.
- The grant sits outside the 15% employee pool.
- Vest over 36 months in equal monthly installments.
- Use a six-month cliff as the recommended compromise. At the cliff, the accrued six months vest; vesting then continues monthly.
- Start vesting when the CEO begins substantive formation work, even if payroll or formal incorporation occurs later.
- Permit voting and other ordinary stockholder rights while shares remain subject to repurchase, subject to counsel's structure.
- Repurchase only unvested shares at original purchase price when service ends.
- Keep vested shares owned by the CEO, subject to normal transfer restrictions, right of first refusal, tag-along, and drag-along terms.

If restricted stock is used, U.S. tax counsel should address a potential Section 83(b) election. The IRS states that Form 15620 or a written election must be filed no later than 30 days after the property transfer. See the [IRS Form 15620 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15620.pdf).

### 3.4 CEO dilution protection

The phrase "anti-dilution" should be converted into the following specific protections.

**Before a Qualified Financing:**

- The CEO remains at 25% fully diluted after the full $4.0M Sponsor commitment is funded.
- Grants from the original 15% pool do not change the stated formation cap table because that pool is already included.
- Sponsor-affiliate issuances, new convertible instruments, or an increase in the employee pool require CEO consent or a corrective top-up in addition to reserved-matter approval.
- An unauthorized issuance that reduces the CEO below the agreed percentage triggers a corrective top-up, subject to counsel's drafting.
- Stock splits, combinations, and recapitalizations receive automatic equitable adjustment.

For this purpose, a **Qualified Financing** means at least $2.0M of gross new cash from one or more unaffiliated third-party investors, at arm's-length pricing, approved by the board including the independent director, and issued in a bona fide equity financing. Sponsor or affiliate purchases, bridge loans, nominal third-party issuances, and ordinary employee grants do not qualify.

**After a Qualified Financing:**

- Sponsor, CEO, and employee holders dilute proportionately on an as-converted basis.
- The CEO receives a pro rata or pre-emptive right to buy sufficient securities to maintain the CEO's ownership percentage, subject to customary exceptions.
- Any increase to the employee pool dilutes all pre-financing holders proportionately unless expressly agreed otherwise.
- The CEO receives the same price-based protection as other purchasers only when buying the same class of security.

Any pre-Qualified-Financing top-up should be issued by NewCo. The fraction corresponding to the CEO's then-vested percentage vests immediately; the balance follows the remaining original vesting schedule. Counsel must address tax and securities treatment before issuance.

A permanent, no-cost 25% floor through every future institutional financing is likely to obstruct financing. The priority is protection against dilution caused by the already-promised Sponsor funding, affiliate issuances, or pool manipulation.

### 3.5 Employee equity pool

- Reserve 15% at formation on a fully diluted basis.
- The CEO recommends grants within a board-approved compensation framework.
- The board approves grants; Managing Partners should not make direct equity promises to employees.
- Pool increases require reserved-matter approval and dilute existing holders proportionately.
- The annual budget should include an equity plan administrator and required valuation work.

### 3.6 Transfer, sale, and information rights

- The CEO receives full tag-along rights if the Sponsor sells control and pro rata tag rights on other material Sponsor secondary sales.
- A drag-along should require at least 75% of the voting equity plus board approval, so the Sponsor's 60% alone cannot force a sale.
- All dragged holders receive the same form and amount of consideration per share, subject only to differences among valid security classes.
- CEO representations in a sale should be limited to title, authority, and the CEO's own conduct. Liability should be several, not joint, and capped at sale proceeds actually received.
- The CEO should not be required to sign a new non-compete as part of a drag unless it is lawful and separately compensated.
- The CEO receives quarterly financial statements, annual accounts, budgets, cap-table updates, and reasonable inspection rights while holding at least 5% fully diluted.

### 4.2 Bonus definitions

The bonus plan must define the financial terms before the year begins.

| Term | Definition for this framework |
|---|---|
| Bookings / TCV | Signed contract value, including future periods; a sales KPI, not earned revenue |
| Total or gross revenue | Revenue recognized under the approved accounting policy before the plan's net-revenue deductions; excludes sales taxes, refunds, and credits |
| Net revenue | A fixed management metric equal to total revenue less approved third-party pass-throughs, reimbursed expenses, reseller amounts, refunds, credits, and similar items |
| Gross profit | Net revenue less direct delivery labor, subcontractors, hosting, licenses, and other direct delivery costs |
| Adjusted EBITDA | Gross profit less board-approved operating expenses, using a pre-agreed adjustment schedule |
| Collections | Cash received against invoices due; excludes intercompany offsets and uncollected accruals |

These are management-plan definitions. The controller and external accountant must separately determine principal-versus-agent presentation and the applicable accounting treatment for pass-through amounts.

### 4.3 Single-metric bonus alternatives

If the parties want a simple one-metric plan, use one of the following. Payouts interpolate linearly and remain subject to the gates below.

| Bonus basis | Threshold | Target | Stretch | Maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Total/gross recognized revenue | $4.4M = $50K | $5.5M = $100K | $6.9M = $150K | $8.25M = $200K |
| Net revenue | $4.0M = $50K | $5.0M = $100K | $6.25M = $150K | $7.5M = $200K |
| Gross profit | $2.0M = $50K | $2.5M = $100K | $3.125M = $150K | $3.75M = $200K |

Of the single metrics, gross profit is the best choice. Total revenue can reward large but low-margin transactions. Net revenue improves the measure but still does not protect delivery economics.

### 4.4 Recommended balanced bonus

Use a $100,000 target and $200,000 maximum with the following weights:

| Metric | Weight | Year 1 target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net revenue | 25% | $5.0M |
| Gross profit dollars | 30% | $2.5M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 20% | $0.3M |
| Collection rate on invoices due | 15% | 95% |
| Business-build milestones | 10% | Board-approved closing, hiring, sales, delivery-hub, and operating-control milestones |
| **Total** | **100%** |  |

Use this complete payout matrix. Percentages are payout factors applied to each metric's weighted share of the $100,000 target bonus.

| Metric | Below threshold: 0% | Threshold: 50% | Target: 100% | Stretch: 150% | Maximum: 200% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Net revenue | Below $4.0M | $4.0M | $5.0M | $6.25M | $7.5M |
| Gross profit | Below $2.0M | $2.0M | $2.5M | $3.125M | $3.75M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | Below $0 | $0 | $0.3M | $0.75M | $1.25M |
| Collections of invoices due | Below 90% | 90% | 95% | 97.5% | 99% |

Interpolate linearly between the stated points. The overall bonus cannot exceed $200,000.

The five business-build milestones are:

1. Entity, funding, governance, insurance, banking, payroll, and accounting are live.
2. The board-approved initial leadership and offshore-hub organization is operational.
3. Agent Ramp/HSG agreements, account schedules, CRM rules, and the intercompany rate card are operating.
4. At least $1.5M of signed backlog is in place by Day 90.
5. NewCo produces a clean monthly close, forecast, related-party reconciliation, and board pack on schedule for three consecutive months.

The milestone component pays 0% for zero to two completed milestones, 50% for three, 100% for four, and 200% for all five.

Apply these protections:

- No credit for unsigned bookings.
- Related-party revenue counts only under a written, board-approved agreement and only if due amounts are collected by the bonus-payment date.
- Pass-through revenue receives no net-revenue or gross-profit credit.
- Credits, refunds, bad debt, and write-offs reduce the applicable metric.
- Revenue-based metrics score zero if company gross margin is below 40%, unless the board approved a strategic exception before contract signature.
- The bonus plan and targets must be approved within 30 days after the fiscal year begins and cannot be changed retroactively without CEO consent.
- If the Sponsor misses the Section 8 obligation, the bonus net-revenue target is reduced by the eligible Sponsor-channel revenue shortfall, but not below $2.0M; the gross-profit target is reduced by the eligible gross-profit shortfall, but not below $1.15M. Independent-director approval is required, and the adjustment cannot be withheld because the Sponsor disputes its own default.
- The final calculation is delivered within 60 days after year-end and paid within 15 days after delivery. If payment would leave less than four months of fixed operating cash, the earned amount may be deferred until 30 days after the cash floor is restored, but no longer than 12 months without CEO consent. A Good Leaver remains entitled to the earned amount.

### 4.5 Termination, severance, and vesting

**Good Leaver** should include termination without Cause, resignation for Good Reason, death or disability, role elimination after a transaction, Sponsor failure to fund, or a material breach of the CEO agreements.

**Cause** should be limited to a felony conviction or plea; fraud, theft, embezzlement, or serious dishonesty; gross negligence or willful misconduct causing material harm; a material policy or agreement breach that remains uncured for 30 days where curable; or repeated willful refusal to follow a lawful written board directive within the agreed governance structure. It should not include missed forecasts, good-faith decisions, personality conflict, strategic disagreement, or ordinary negligence. Cause requires written particulars, an opportunity for the CEO and counsel to respond, and a determination by disinterested directors.

**Good Reason** should include a material reduction in title, authority, duties, reporting status, base salary, or target bonus; material relocation; operating interference contrary to the delegation matrix; or breach of the funding, employment, or equity agreements. The CEO gives notice within 90 days after learning of the event, NewCo receives 30 days to cure, and the CEO resigns within 90 days after a failure to cure.

Recommended consequences:

| Event | Cash treatment | Equity treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Without Cause / Good Reason | 12 months of base salary, prorated earned bonus, and benefits continuation where lawful | 12 months of additional vesting |
| Death / permanent disability | Prorated earned bonus and agreed benefits | At least 12 months of acceleration |
| Voluntary resignation without Good Reason | Earned salary and approved expenses | CEO keeps vested shares; unvested shares are repurchased |
| Cause | Earned salary and lawful entitlements | CEO keeps vested shares, except a narrowly drafted fraud or theft remedy; unvested shares are repurchased |

For a change of control, use 100% double-trigger acceleration if the CEO is terminated without Cause or resigns for Good Reason within 12-18 months. A reasonable opening request is also 12 months or 25%-50% single-trigger acceleration at closing.

Severance may be conditioned on a customary release that is delivered promptly, does not waive vested equity or indemnification, and becomes irrevocable within a defined period. The employment agreement should state payment timing, benefits mechanics, tax treatment, and the effect of any payment delay required by law.

## 5. Governance, authority, and roles

### 5.1 Board structure

Recommended five-member board:

- Three Sponsor nominees.
- The Founding CEO.
- One independent director mutually approved by the Sponsor and CEO.

The Sponsor retains ordinary control consistent with its 60% ownership. The independent director provides credible oversight for conflicts and related-party transactions.

Additional provisions:

- Sponsor selects the chair; the chair has no casting vote.
- CEO retains the board seat while serving as CEO and holding at least 5% fully diluted.
- Quorum requires at least one Sponsor director and either the CEO or independent director, with an adjourned-meeting mechanism to prevent tactical absence.
- Ordinary matters pass by simple majority.
- Reserved matters require four of five directors and, where a conflict exists, approval by the independent or other disinterested director.
- Amendments that specifically and adversely affect the CEO's equity, vesting, employment economics, or dilution protection require CEO consent.

### 5.2 Founding CEO mandate

The CEO is accountable for:

- Company strategy and execution.
- Full P&L, cash, working capital, and forecast ownership.
- Sales, pricing, proposals, and deal structuring in partnership with Managing Partners.
- Building the solutions and delivery organization.
- Establishing any required product organization only at the level of business structure, staffing, budget, and P&L; product strategy is outside this document.
- Establishing and managing the offshore delivery hub.
- Hiring, performance management, organization design, and culture.
- Customer delivery quality, account growth, and collections.
- Partnerships, sales channels, and contracting vehicles.
- Risk, compliance, insurance, and operational controls.
- Monthly management reporting and quarterly board reporting.

Within the approved budget and risk framework, the CEO has full day-to-day authority. Individual Managing Partners do not direct NewCo employees or commit NewCo to a price, scope, deadline, hire, or expense.

### 5.3 Base leadership roles

The Base Plan assumes the following operating shape by Month 12. Hiring remains staged against signed work and cash visibility.

| Role | Primary business accountability |
|---|---|
| Founding CEO | Strategy, P&L, sales, deal approval, organization, capital, and board reporting |
| Head of Sales / Business Development | Pipeline, account plans, CRM discipline, pursuits, and bookings |
| Head of Solutions and Delivery | Scoping, staffing, margin, delivery accountability, and quality |
| Business Capability Lead | Build the reusable business capability and operating enablement approved in the annual plan |
| Finance and Operations Lead | Close, budget, cash, controls, payroll, intercompany accounting, and reporting |
| Commercial / Program Lead | Proposals, contract-to-delivery handoff, program controls, and change orders |
| Customer Success / Account Lead | Account health, renewals, expansions, references, and collection support |
| Offshore Hub Lead plus five delivery staff | Capacity, utilization, local operations, delivery performance, and workforce management |

Functional leaders report to the CEO. Managing Partners may sponsor accounts and mentor leaders but do not create a parallel reporting line.

### 5.4 Managing Partner and Sponsor obligations

The Managing Partners and Sponsor are accountable for:

- Funding the $4.0M capital commitment on schedule.
- Supplying the commercial commitments in Section 8.
- Providing executive introductions, access to named accounts, and active support on strategic pursuits.
- Participating in board governance and major strategic decisions.
- Identifying existing contracts, sales vehicles, relationships, and resources that may legally support NewCo.
- Respecting the CEO's operating authority and using the agreed escalation path.
- Disclosing conflicts and recusing where required.
- Ensuring affiliate work is documented, priced, invoiced, and approved under the intercompany framework.

Board service, shareholder oversight, and the Managing Partners' required Year 1 commercial support are not billable to NewCo unless the board explicitly approves a separate client-facing scope.

### 5.5 Delegation and approval matrix

The board should refresh thresholds annually. The opening delegation is:

| Decision | CEO authority | Board / committee approval |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring | All non-C-suite hires within approved headcount and budget | C-suite hires, equity grants, or cash compensation above $225K |
| Customer pricing | Within approved rate bands and at least 45% expected gross margin | Exceptions between 35%-45% require the designated commercial committee; below 35% requires full board approval |
| Customer contracts | Up to $1.0M TCV on approved risk terms | Above $1.0M TCV or any exceptional liability, exclusivity, guarantee, or material non-standard risk |
| Vendor commitments | Budgeted commitments up to $100K annual value | Above $100K or outside plan |
| Unbudgeted spend | Up to the lower of $50K or 2% of annual operating expense in aggregate per quarter | Above threshold |
| Related-party SOW | Within approved MSA, rate card, budget, and margin rules | Master terms, exceptions, or material conflicts; interested directors recuse |
| Debt | Ordinary trade credit and approved facilities | Borrowing, guarantees, or liens above $250K |
| Organization | Day-to-day design and performance decisions | Removal of CEO; material change to CEO mandate |
| Capital and ownership | Recommend financing | All equity, convertible securities, pool increases, dividends, or repurchases |
| Major transactions | Recommend | Acquisition, sale, merger, liquidation, or material asset transfer |

### 5.6 Reserved matters

Reserved matters include:

- Issuing, repurchasing, or changing rights attached to securities.
- Increasing or repricing the employee pool.
- Amending the charter, bylaws, or stockholders agreement.
- Approving or materially changing the annual plan or budget.
- Incurring material debt, guarantees, liens, or unbudgeted capital expenditure.
- Entering a merger, acquisition, asset sale, liquidation, or dividend.
- Entering or amending material related-party transactions.
- Appointing or removing the CEO or making a Cause determination.
- Entering material litigation settlements or regulatory admissions.
- Changing auditors, material accounting policies, fiscal year, or revenue-recognition policies.
- Taking an action that disproportionately changes the CEO's agreed equity or economic rights.

### 6.2 Recommended Base Plan: $4.0M

The Base Plan is recommended because the CEO is expected to build a complete commercial, solutions, delivery, operating, and offshore capability rather than merely act as an individual seller.

The $4.0M comprises approximately:

- $2.881M planned first-year cash spend.
- $0.288M contingency.
- $0.600M ring-fenced project working capital.
- $0.231M rounding buffer for jurisdiction, insurance, tax, hiring, and timing variance.

The working-capital reserve should not be treated as ordinary discretionary spending. It protects NewCo from customer payment terms, payroll timing, and project mobilization.

The Base cash plan reserves the CEO's target bonus plus normal sales-variable compensation. The CEO's maximum bonus becomes payable only when upside performance produces the corresponding economics and cash; it is not assumed in the Base spend.

The opening capital finances the fixed team, launch costs, planned contractor capacity, contingency, and peak working capital. It does not pre-fund every variable delivery dollar associated with the full $5.0M revenue target. Customer deposits, milestone billing, and collections must fund project-specific direct costs beyond the planned capacity.

### 6.3 Funding mechanics

Preferred approach: fund $4.0M at closing.

Acceptable alternative:

- $3.0M funded at closing.
- $1.0M irrevocably committed and funded automatically before unrestricted cash falls below six months of forecast fixed operating spend, and no later than the end of Month 6.
- The final tranche is not subject to discretionary board reapproval or a revised valuation.

Additional rules:

- Revenue extends runway but does not reduce the Sponsor's legal commitment.
- A customer engagement requiring more than $600K peak cash exposure needs a deposit, milestone billing, or separate approved deal financing.
- Management begins a financing or cost-reset plan when forecast unrestricted runway falls below nine months.
- Cash may not fall below six months of approved fixed operating spend without a board-approved corrective plan.
- Sponsor funding default after notice and cure constitutes Good Reason for the CEO and permits the board to seek alternative financing.

| **Total cost against $5.5M gross revenue** | **$5.200M** | Produces $0.300M adjusted EBITDA |

## 8. Agent Ramp and HSG Year 1 commercial structure

### 8.1 Founding Commercial Partner Agreements

NewCo should execute a separate Founding Commercial Partner Agreement with Agent Ramp and HSG. Each agreement should contain:

- Named accounts and existing-account exclusions.
- Rules for renewals, expansions, and new buying centers.
- Required Managing Partner introductions and executive sponsorship.
- Opportunity-registration and CRM requirements.
- Service levels for bid/no-bid, pricing, proposal support, and approvals.
- Permitted deal models: referral, NewCo prime, affiliate prime/NewCo subcontract, and reseller/channel.
- Revenue, margin, cash, and pipeline commitments.
- Rate-card and fee rules.
- No exclusivity unless backed by a binding minimum purchase or revenue guarantee.
- Annual renewal and quarterly performance review.
- No authority for an affiliate to bind NewCo.

### 8.2 Recommended Year 1 sponsor commitment

The obligation is cumulative, not either/or. NewCo must receive:

1. At least $3.0M of Eligible Sponsor-Channel Net Revenue recognized by NewCo in Year 1.
2. At least $1.35M of gross profit on that revenue.
3. At least 95% collection of the related invoices that are due by the measurement date.

"Eligible Sponsor-Channel Net Revenue" means NewCo revenue from named Sponsor-supported accounts, determined under the approved accounting policy, excluding pass-throughs, credits, taxes, unapproved related-party entries, and the affiliates' end-customer billings when NewCo is only a subcontractor. Gross profit is calculated under Section 4.2. Affiliate fees are then included in the contribution-margin test in Section 8.4.

Illustrative allocation, to be replaced with the parties' real account data:

| Sponsor channel | Year 1 net-revenue commitment |
|---|---:|
| Agent Ramp | $1.8M |
| HSG | $1.2M |
| **Combined** | **$3.0M** |

The definitive documents must name the legal obligors. Recommended structure:

- Agent Ramp signs for its $1.8M allocation.
- HSG signs for its $1.2M allocation.
- Sponsor HoldCo guarantees both obligations jointly and severally.
- Individual Managing Partners are not guarantors unless they sign a separate guarantee.
- If Agent Ramp, HSG, and Sponsor HoldCo are not separate entities, counsel consolidates the obligations without weakening the guarantee.

Milestones:

| Date | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Day 30 | Commercial-partner agreements, account schedules, rate card, and CRM rules signed |
| Day 90 | At least $1.5M under signed customer contracts or subcontract SOWs |
| Day 180 | Full $3.0M in contracted backlog |
| Year end | $3.0M eligible net revenue, at least $1.35M gross profit, and at least 95% of invoices due collected |

Maintain at least three times pipeline coverage against the remaining bookings target. At the beginning of the year, that implies approximately $15M of qualified total pipeline, including at least $9M tied to Sponsor-led or Sponsor-supported accounts.

Use cumulative quarterly net-revenue targets of $0.45M, $1.20M, $2.04M, and $3.00M. The corresponding gross-profit targets are 45% of those amounts.

At each quarter-end, the automatic non-dilutive Sponsor shortfall payment is:

`Payment_q = max(0, max[45% x max(0, cumulative net-revenue target - cumulative actual eligible net revenue), max(0, cumulative gross-profit target - cumulative actual gross profit)] - prior shortfall payments).`

NewCo issues the calculation within 10 business days after quarter-end. The named obligors may review the records but must fund an undisputed amount within 15 business days. Any dispute goes to the independent director and then an agreed independent accountant; the undisputed amount is paid while the dispute is resolved. Failure to pay after a 10-business-day cure is a Sponsor funding default and triggers debt-enforcement, alternative-financing, and CEO Good-Reason remedies. Shortfall payments are additional paid-in capital with no extra equity.

Affiliate-prime invoices owed directly to NewCo and more than 30 days overdue are funded dollar-for-dollar by the applicable affiliate and are not deferred until the annual test.

### 8.3 If a hard $3.0M guarantee is not feasible

Use all of these protections:

1. A binding $1.5M minimum purchase or subcontract commitment at no less than 45% gross margin.
2. An additional $0.675M non-dilutive commercial-risk reserve funded at closing, increasing total committed capital from $4.0M to $4.675M.
3. The same named-obligor, payment, cure, and audit mechanics described above.
4. Release of the reserve from its ring fence only as NewCo earns the missing $0.675M of gross profit from other Sponsor-supported accounts.

Pipeline and introductions alone are not a substitute for either a revenue commitment or committed operating capital.

### 8.4 Sponsor and affiliate economics

The first $3.0M of required sponsor-channel net revenue is part of the Sponsor's formation commitment. No referral, pursuit, or pure prime-administration fee applies to that floor unless it is expressly scheduled at signing and the $1.35M gross-profit and 40% contribution-margin tests remain satisfied. Affiliate delivery performed under the rate card remains a direct delivery cost.

For business above the commitment:

| Contribution | Recommended economics |
|---|---|
| Qualified introduction only | 5% of first-year collected net revenue; no fee on pass-throughs |
| Active co-sell and pursuit work | Approved recorded hours, or 7.5%-10% of first-year collected net revenue instead of a referral fee |
| Affiliate is prime; NewCo is subcontractor | NewCo invoices its agreed subcontract value; affiliate may retain an agreed 5%-8% prime-administration amount where justified |
| NewCo is prime; affiliate supplies delivery | Affiliate invoices under the approved rate card or cost-plus formula |
| Reseller/channel | Pre-agreed discount tied to actual channel responsibility and collection risk |

Do not pay a full referral fee, pursuit hours, and a delivery markup for the same contribution without disinterested approval.

For every deal, calculate contribution margin after all direct delivery costs, affiliate referral or pursuit fees, retained prime amounts, reseller discounts, and unreimbursed pursuit costs. The normal minimum contribution margin is 40% of net revenue. A 30%-40% exception requires the designated commercial committee; anything below 30% requires full board approval. All percentage fees use collected net revenue excluding taxes, credits, pass-throughs, and reimbursed expenses.

## 9. Separate P&L and intercompany operating model

### 9.1 Entity separation

NewCo must maintain its own:

- Bank and treasury accounts.
- General ledger, chart of accounts, monthly close, and tax records.
- Payroll, benefits, expenses, and employment records.
- Customer and vendor contracts.
- CRM, pipeline, proposals, and customer records.
- Insurance program.
- Invoicing, collections, accounts payable, and accounts receivable.
- Policies, approval limits, timekeeping, and project accounting.

No affiliate should pay NewCo expenses directly except as a documented capital contribution, loan, reimbursable payment, or intercompany service.

### 9.2 Revenue and expense ownership

| Scenario | Contracting entity | Accounting treatment |
|---|---|---|
| NewCo primes and controls the work | NewCo | NewCo records customer revenue and affiliate subcontract expense |
| Agent Ramp or HSG primes | Affiliate | Affiliate records customer revenue; NewCo records only its subcontract revenue |
| Pure referral | Entity signing the customer contract | Contracting entity records revenue and the approved referral fee as selling expense |
| Shared personnel | Employing entity | Receiving entity records intercompany service expense under an SOW |
| Shared systems/facilities | Entity holding vendor contract | Receiving entity records usage-based allocated expense |
| Sponsor funding | Sponsor / NewCo | Equity or debt according to documents; never operating revenue |

The entity named in the customer contract should normally control scope, bear contractual risk, invoice, and collect. It records revenue only to the extent permitted by the applicable principal-versus-agent accounting analysis; contract name or co-branding is not by itself determinative. The controller documents that analysis before the first invoice for every reseller, affiliate-prime, or material pass-through model.

### 9.3 Intercompany agreements

Use an Intercompany Services Framework Agreement with each affiliate and a separate SOW for every material engagement. Each SOW should state:

- Scope, deliverables, and acceptance.
- Named personnel, location, hours, and rates.
- Expenses and taxes.
- Customer-contract obligations that validly flow down.
- Confidentiality, data, and business-asset treatment.
- Liability, insurance, change control, and dispute handling.
- Invoice timing, payment terms, and audit support.

Execute the SOW before work begins. Require time or usage records, invoice monthly, use net-30 terms, reconcile balances monthly, and report affiliate revenue, spend, receivables, payables, and margin in the board pack.

### 9.4 Initial intercompany rate card

These are opening internal transfer rates, not the customer-facing price list. Finance should benchmark them annually.

| Role | Hourly ceiling | Day ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|
| Managing Partner / Executive | $450 | $3,600 |
| Principal / Industry SME | $325 | $2,600 |
| Deal or Solution Architect | $275 | $2,200 |
| Program Director | $250 | $2,000 |
| Engagement Manager | $210 | $1,680 |
| Senior Consultant / Business Analyst | $175 | $1,400 |
| Consultant / Analyst | $135 | $1,080 |
| Offshore Delivery Lead | $95 | $760 |
| Offshore Senior Specialist | $70 | $560 |
| Offshore Analyst / Delivery Professional | $50 | $400 |

Default pricing rules:

- Delivery services: fully burdened cost plus 15%, capped by the table unless approved otherwise.
- Routine finance, HR, payroll, or administrative support: fully burdened cost plus 10%.
- Third-party expenses: documented actual cost, normally without markup.
- Shared systems or facilities: documented usage allocation plus no more than a modest administration charge.
- Board, shareholder, and required Sponsor commercial-support time: not billable.
- Customer-facing pricing: set independently to preserve NewCo's approved margin, not simply passed through from this table.

For cross-border services, finance and tax advisers must validate the cost base, markup, withholding, VAT/GST, permanent-establishment exposure, and transfer-pricing support. The IRS arm's-length principle for controlled services emphasizes comparable independent pricing and adequate records; see the [IRS Section 482 regulations](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-apa/482_regs.pdf).

### 9.5 Opportunity attribution

All opportunities must be registered in NewCo's CRM before material pursuit work starts. Record:

- Source and originator.
- Account owner and executive sponsor.
- Proposed contracting entity.
- Expected contract value, net revenue, gross profit, and cash profile.
- Pursuit responsibilities and delivery owner.
- Affiliate contribution and proposed economics.
- Conflicts, existing-account exclusions, and next action.

No retroactive reclassification is permitted merely to improve a party's revenue, bonus, or margin. Unresolved conflicts go to the CEO and joint GTM council, then to the independent director or disinterested board committee.

It also states that standard MAS eligibility generally includes two years in business, two years of financial statements, and measurable past performance. As of this draft, GSA's Startup Springboard route is limited to qualifying IT offers participating in FASt Lane and sponsored by an agency. ## 15. Negotiation priorities

### Founding CEO must-haves

1. The 25% grant is issued at formation, fully diluted, and outside the employee pool.
2. The $4.0M Sponsor commitment is binding, included in the Sponsor's 60%, and non-dilutive.
3. Vesting is exactly 36 months, with no more than a six-month cliff and credit for formation work.
4. Cause is narrow; Good Reason includes funding failure and authority erosion.
5. Severance and meaningful vesting acceleration apply for a Good Leaver and change of control.
6. CEO has a board seat and full operating authority within the approved plan.
7. Affiliate work uses written, arm's-length terms and disinterested approval.
8. Sponsor revenue support is measurable and backed by signed backlog or cash protection.
9. Pool expansion, affiliate issuances, and extra Sponsor equity require reserved approval.
10. CEO receives tag-along, information, and pro rata participation rights.

### Reasonable fallbacks

- Six-month cliff instead of no cliff.
- Pro rata rights after third-party financing instead of a permanent no-cost 25% floor.
- Double-trigger acceleration instead of full single-trigger acceleration.
- Three Sponsor board seats if the independent director has real related-party approval authority.
- $3.0M at closing plus an irrevocable $1.0M automatic tranche instead of all $4.0M cash at closing.
- A $1.5M minimum purchase plus the $0.675M non-dilutive commercial-risk reserve if the full $3.0M guarantee is not feasible.

### Avoid conceding

- A future promise of equity rather than an issued grant.
- Sponsor shares without a binding funding obligation.
- Further dilution for the already-approved first-year capital.
- Repurchase of vested CEO shares at cost for ordinary departure.
- Broad Cause, discretionary forfeiture, or performance-based clawback of vested equity.
- A bonus that can be changed retroactively or based on undefined "revenue."
- Informal affiliate use of NewCo people, contracts, cash, or customer relationships.
- Sponsor referral fees and delivery markup on the required revenue floor without explicit economic adjustment.
- Personal guarantees by the CEO.
