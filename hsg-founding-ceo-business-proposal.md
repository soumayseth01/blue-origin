# HSG Sponsor-Backed NewCo — Founding CEO Business Proposal

**Status:** Negotiation draft  
**Date:** July 27, 2026  
**Currency:** U.S. dollars  
**Scope:** Ownership, CEO mandate, operating model, team, funding, commercial relationship, and Year 1 execution. Product strategy and board structure are intentionally excluded.

> This is a non-binding business proposal, not legal, tax, employment, accounting, or investment advice. Definitive agreements should be prepared by counsel in the relevant U.S., Canadian, and Indian jurisdictions.

## 1. Proposal at a glance

HSG will sponsor a new, independently operated company ("NewCo"). HSG will hold 60% of the fully diluted equity, the Founding CEO will hold 25%, and 15% will be reserved for employees. The Founding CEO will report to the HSG Sponsors and will have full authority for day-to-day operations within the agreed annual plan and budget.

The recommended Year 1 capital commitment is **$2.0M without a full-time Canada CTO** or **$2.2M if the Canada CTO joins at the outset**. This replaces the earlier $4.0M assumption. The revised amount is based on the India-heavy delivery structure and loaded role costs in the WorkspaceAR People Rate Card.

| Term | Proposed position |
|---|---|
| HSG Sponsors | 60% fully diluted |
| Founding CEO | 25% fully diluted, outside the employee pool |
| Employee equity pool | 15% fully diluted, reserved at formation |
| CEO reporting | Founding CEO reports to the HSG Sponsors |
| Day-to-day authority | Founding CEO |
| CEO base salary | $200,000 annually |
| CEO bonus | $100,000 target; $200,000 maximum |
| CEO vesting | 36 months; six-month delivery cliff; monthly thereafter |
| Year 1 Sponsor capital | $2.0M base commitment; $2.2M with full-time Canada CTO |
| Delivery model | Customer-facing leadership plus an India Engineering Lead and India delivery pods |
| CTO structure | Canada CTO if available; India-led contingency if not |
| P&L | NewCo maintains separate books, bank accounts, contracts, payroll, and financial reporting |
| HSG/Agent Ramp work | Written SOW, rate card, time records, invoicing, and transfer-pricing rules |

## 2. Company ownership

### 2.1 Opening capitalization

The ownership percentages should be stated on a **fully diluted basis**, including the entire employee pool from formation.

| Holder | Fully diluted ownership | Illustrative shares on a 10M-share basis |
|---|---:|---:|
| HSG Sponsor entity | 60% | 6,000,000 |
| Founding CEO | 25% | 2,500,000 |
| Employee pool, reserved and ungranted | 15% | 1,500,000 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **10,000,000** |

The HSG interests should be held through one identified Sponsor entity. The internal ownership of that Sponsor entity is an HSG matter and should not complicate NewCo's capitalization or operating relationship.

### 2.2 Founding CEO equity protection

The CEO's 25% grant should be documented at formation, not promised for a later date. It should sit outside the 15% employee pool.

The following dilution rules are recommended:

- The full Year 1 Sponsor capital commitment and the original 15% employee pool are already reflected in the opening percentages and do not cause additional CEO dilution.
- HSG cannot receive extra equity for funding the agreed Year 1 commitment.
- Before a bona fide third-party financing, new Sponsor or affiliate issuances, convertible instruments, or an increase to the employee pool require the CEO's written consent or an appropriate corrective top-up.
- A genuine third-party financing dilutes HSG, the CEO, and the employee pool proportionately.
- The CEO receives a pro-rata participation right in future equity financings, subject to customary legal exceptions.

This is a practical anti-dilution framework. A permanent, cost-free 25% floor through every future financing is not recommended because it can make external financing difficult.

## 3. Founding CEO vesting

### 3.1 Recommended schedule

The CEO's 25% grant vests over 36 months with a six-month cliff linked to an objective initial delivery milestone.

| Date | Vesting event | CEO grant vested | Company equity vested |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Months 1–5 | Accrues, but remains unvested | 0% | 0% |
| End of Month 6 | Initial Delivery Milestone satisfied; six months vest together | 16.667% | 4.167% |
| Months 7–36 | 1/36 of the CEO grant vests each month | 2.778% per month | 0.694% per month |
| End of Year 1 | Cumulative | 33.333% | 8.333% |
| End of Year 2 | Cumulative | 66.667% | 16.667% |
| End of Year 3 | Fully vested | 100% | 25.000% |

### 3.2 Initial Delivery Milestone

The cliff should not depend on a subjective statement that a project was "successful." It should be satisfied when all of the following objective conditions have occurred:

1. At least one customer or HSG/Agent Ramp delivery SOW has been executed and funded.
2. HSG has provided the agreed capital, access, approvals, and resources required for delivery.
3. NewCo has completed the first material contracted milestone within the CEO's control.
4. The milestone has been accepted by the customer or deemed accepted under the SOW, and there is no material uncured delivery breach attributable to the CEO.

If no qualifying project is made available, the funding tranche is delayed, a customer delays acceptance, or another dependency outside the CEO's reasonable control prevents completion, the condition should be deemed satisfied at the end of Month 6. The CEO should not lose vesting because HSG did not provide an opportunity or because a customer delayed a decision.

If the CEO is terminated without cause or resigns for a defined good reason, six months of additional vesting is recommended. A sale of NewCo followed by termination without cause within 12 months should accelerate all remaining vesting. Definitive documents should define cause, good reason, acceptance, and change of control precisely.

## 4. Founding CEO mandate

The Founding CEO reports to the HSG Sponsors and is accountable for the complete business result. The role includes:

- Company formation, operating launch, annual plan, and execution.
- P&L ownership, cash management, forecasting, pricing discipline, and collections.
- Sales leadership and deal structuring jointly with HSG Managing Partners.
- Building and managing the solutions, delivery, engineering, and customer-success organization.
- Establishing and leading the India delivery hub.
- Recruiting the Canada CTO if the agreed candidate joins, or executing the contingency model if the candidate does not join.
- Hiring, performance management, compensation recommendations, and allocation of the 15% employee pool.
- Customer delivery, quality, risk, and executive escalation.
- Establishing independent NewCo finance, contracting, HR, insurance, and compliance processes.
- Selecting the sales vehicle for each opportunity and negotiating the associated economics.

The CEO owns day-to-day decisions, including customer pursuit, pricing within agreed margin limits, staffing, vendors, delivery methods, and expenditures within the approved budget. HSG Managing Partners should not directly assign work to NewCo employees; Sponsor requests and priorities flow through the CEO.

## 5. Sponsor responsibilities

HSG's 60% ownership carries four concrete Year 1 responsibilities.

### 5.1 Capital

HSG provides a legally committed 12-month runway of:

- **$2.0M** under the India-led base plan; or
- **$2.2M** if the full-time Canada CTO joins at the beginning of Year 1.

The commitment is gross funding, not a promise to cover only losses after expected revenue. Collected revenue remains in NewCo and extends runway.

### 5.2 Commercial access

HSG Managing Partners will actively create access to clients, accounts, teaming partners, and contracting vehicles. The proposed minimum commercial commitment is:

- $750,000 of signed NewCo SOW value within the first 90 days; and
- $1.5M of cumulative HSG- or Agent Ramp-sourced signed NewCo SOW value during Year 1.

This can be met through direct NewCo contracts, HSG prime/NewCo subcontract work, or HSG/Agent Ramp purchasing legitimate delivery services from NewCo. Pipeline, introductions, or non-binding proposals do not count as signed SOW value.

### 5.3 Sponsor participation in sales

HSG Managing Partners will:

- Open executive relationships and provide account context.
- Participate in opportunity qualification and executive-level sales meetings.
- Support commercial credibility, references, and partner introductions.
- Make Sponsor decisions within two business days when a live deal requires them.
- Avoid committing NewCo scope, staffing, price, delivery dates, or warranties without CEO approval.

### 5.4 Sponsor operating discipline

HSG will provide accurate information, execute affiliate agreements, disclose conflicts, and avoid shifting HSG or Agent Ramp costs into NewCo without a written SOW. Sponsor ownership does not itself create a management fee, referral fee, or right to use NewCo employees without charge.

## 6. Operating relationship: CEO and HSG Sponsors

The working model is deliberately simple.

| Area | Founding CEO | HSG Sponsors |
|---|---|---|
| Daily operations | Final authority | Informed through reporting cadence |
| Hiring and team allocation | Final authority within budget | Consulted on executive hires |
| Sales pursuit | Leads qualification, solution, pricing, and close | Opens doors and joins strategic pursuits |
| Deal structure | Recommends and negotiates | Approves related-party terms and exceptions to agreed risk limits |
| Delivery | Accountable for staffing, quality, schedule, and margin | Supports executive escalation; does not direct delivery staff |
| Budget and P&L | Prepares, executes, and reports | Funds the commitment and reviews performance |
| Affiliate work | Approves SOW, rate, staffing, and invoice | Ensures HSG/Agent Ramp compliance with the same written process |
| Equity pool | Recommends grants | Confirms grants under definitive documents |

Operating cadence:

- **Weekly:** pipeline, proposals, contracting decisions, and delivery risks.
- **Monthly:** P&L, cash, bookings, backlog, collections, utilization, gross margin, hiring, and major risks.
- **Quarterly:** Year 1 plan progress, capital outlook, compensation, organization capacity, and next-quarter priorities.

Sponsor consent should be limited to exceptional matters such as issuing equity, borrowing outside the plan, selling the company, a related-party transaction outside the agreed rate card, or an unbudgeted commitment above $100,000. This is an authority boundary, not a board structure.

### 7.3 India Engineering Lead mandate

The India Engineering Lead reports to the Canada CTO if appointed; otherwise the role reports directly to the CEO. The Lead owns:

- India hiring recommendations and team allocation.
- Delivery planning, estimates, sprint execution, and release readiness.
- Engineering standards, review discipline, testing, documentation, and operational handoff.
- Coordination across Full-stack, AI/ML, Data, QA, DevOps, and implementation roles.
- Weekly capacity, delivery-risk, and quality reporting.
- Escalation of architecture, security, customer, and staffing decisions.

The India Lead is not merely a project coordinator; the role must have the authority and seniority to run delivery.

### 7.4 Canada CTO scenario

If the CTO candidate joins in Canada:

- The CTO reports to the CEO.
- The CTO owns technical leadership, architecture decisions, engineering standards, security coordination, technical hiring, and mentorship of the India Engineering Lead.
- Day-to-day customer commitments, commercial priorities, budget, and P&L remain with the CEO.
- The planning reserve is **$250,000 loaded cash cost for Year 1**, subject to the actual offer, start date, Canadian employment structure, and any ESOP grant.
- Any CTO equity comes from the 15% employee pool and must be negotiated as part of the offer.

### 7.5 No-CTO contingency

If the Canada CTO does not join:

1. The India Engineering Lead reports directly to the CEO and becomes the operational head of engineering delivery.
2. NewCo retains a fractional Solution Architect for up to 0.5 FTE for six months, budgeted at **$63,000** using the WorkspaceAR U.S. loaded rate.
3. A senior engineer from the India pool is designated technical design owner for each project.
4. Independent security or specialist reviews are purchased only when required by a signed SOW or customer risk profile.
5. A permanent CTO search begins only when signed backlog, customer requirements, or team scale demonstrates the need.

This contingency preserves delivery capacity without leaving a technical-leadership gap or forcing an early executive hire.

### 7.6 Team activation sequence

**At execution of the definitive NewCo and Founding CEO agreements, and receipt of the closing capital:**

- Founding CEO starts.
- India Engineering Lead is appointed or contracted.
- Finance, legal, insurance, payroll/EOR, and contracting infrastructure is activated.
- India candidate availability and rate confirmations are completed.

**At execution of the first funded customer or affiliate SOW:**

- The initial four-person delivery nucleus is activated: Engineering Lead, one Full-stack Engineer, QA Engineer, and fractional DevOps/SRE.
- Remaining pod roles start according to the contracted delivery plan.
- Customer-facing Program & Delivery and Customer Success/FDE allocations are activated.

**As backlog grows:**

- Additional delivery personnel are hired only when at least six months of their loaded cost is covered by signed backlog or the approved operating reserve.
- Contractors may bridge short demand spikes; permanent hiring follows durable backlog.

## 9. Year 1 commercial plan

### 9.1 Financial objectives

The following are operating targets, not substitutes for committed capital:

| Metric | Year 1 target |
|---|---:|
| Signed bookings / SOW value | $3.0M |
| Recognized net revenue | $2.5M |
| Gross profit | $1.25M |
| Gross margin | 50% |
| HSG/Agent Ramp-sourced signed SOW value | At least $1.5M |
| Collection of invoices due | At least 90% |

The normal project pricing floor should be **45% gross margin**, with a 50% target. Exceptions should require a written deal memo explaining the strategic value, cash terms, delivery risk, and path to acceptable economics.

### 9.2 Sales vehicles

Each opportunity should use one of four documented vehicles:

1. **NewCo direct prime:** NewCo contracts with and invoices the customer. NewCo records revenue and owns delivery.
2. **HSG prime / NewCo subcontractor:** HSG contracts with the customer; NewCo signs a subcontract and invoices HSG for defined delivery.
3. **Agent Ramp or affiliate SOW:** The affiliate purchases or supplies defined services under a written intercompany SOW and rate card.
4. **Third-party teaming or subcontract:** NewCo participates through an approved partner where direct qualification or procurement access is unavailable.

The vehicle is selected during opportunity qualification based on customer access, required credentials, margin, risk, cash timing, and ownership of delivery.

### 9.3 Minimum sales qualifications

NewCo should not represent that it can contract until the relevant items are complete:

- Legal formation, tax identification, banking, and authority to contract.
- Commercial general liability, professional liability/errors and omissions, cyber, workers' compensation, and executive insurance appropriate to the work.
- Standard NDA, MSA, SOW, subcontract, DPA, change-order, and acceptance templates.
- Customer onboarding, security questionnaires, data-handling commitments, and vendor due diligence.
- State and local vendor registrations for targeted jurisdictions.
- SAM.gov/UEI and any required representations if federal contracting is pursued.
- Approved pricing, minimum-margin, payment-term, and contracting-risk rules.
- Documented past-performance and key-person qualifications that can be used lawfully.
- A compliant prime or teaming partner when NewCo lacks a required contract vehicle, certification, clearance, or past performance.

No HSG or Agent Ramp qualification, contract vehicle, reference, or customer relationship transfers automatically to NewCo. Its use must be permitted and documented for the specific opportunity.

## 10. CEO compensation and bonus scenarios

The CEO receives a $200,000 base salary. The annual bonus has a $100,000 target and $200,000 maximum. The parties can choose one of the following simple formulas:

| Bonus basis | Formula | $100K target occurs at | Strength / risk |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Total recognized revenue | 2% of collected total revenue | $5.0M | Simple, but may reward pass-through or low-margin work |
| Net revenue | 4% of collected net revenue | $2.5M | Excludes defined pass-throughs; still does not fully protect margin |
| Gross profit — **recommended** | 8% of gross profit | $1.25M | Directly rewards revenue quality and delivery economics |

Each formula is capped at $200,000. "Collected" means cash received for invoices that are due; revenue, net revenue, and gross profit follow a written policy fixed at the start of the year. Intercompany entries, unapproved pass-throughs, sales taxes, credits, and refunds do not inflate the calculation.

Recommended payout protection:

- No cash payout if NewCo cannot maintain three months of payroll after payment.
- Any earned but temporarily deferred amount remains payable when the cash gate is restored.
- No retroactive change to the formula after revenue has been earned.
- Final calculation is provided with the Year 1 financial statements.

## 11. Separate P&L and affiliate rate card

NewCo must operate as a separate business, not as a cost center of HSG or Agent Ramp.

Required controls:

- Separate legal entity, bank account, general ledger, payroll, contracts, insurance, invoicing, accounts receivable, accounts payable, and financial statements.
- Every cross-company service uses a written SOW stating scope, personnel, rate, term, deliverables, acceptance, IP, confidentiality, liability, and payment terms.
- Named resources record time to the correct entity, customer, and project.
- No undocumented expense allocations, free staff sharing, revenue transfers, or end-of-quarter journal entries used to manufacture performance.
- Pre-existing IP, customer contracts, and employees remain with their existing owner unless separately assigned, licensed, or transferred.

### 11.1 Crossover pricing rules

| Situation | Pricing rule |
|---|---|
| Affiliate supplies internal support to NewCo | Verified loaded cost plus 10% administration, unless a lower fixed SOW price is agreed |
| Affiliate supplies customer-delivery labor to NewCo | Agreed role/location rate card with time records and a project cap |
| NewCo supplies delivery under an HSG prime contract | NewCo SOW price based on the customer rate card; any prime discount capped at 15% unless CEO approves the project economics |
| HSG introduces a NewCo-direct customer | 7.5% of collected first-year net revenue from that customer, unless HSG is separately paid for delivery or prime services |
| Shared selling expense | Pre-approved actual cost; no automatic markup |
| Sponsor oversight or shareholder activity | No charge to NewCo |

No fee should be stacked twice. For example, HSG should not receive both a referral fee and a prime-contractor discount for the same economic contribution unless the written deal memo demonstrates separate value.

## 12. First-year execution plan

### Days 0–30: close and establish

- Fund the $900,000 closing tranche.
- Start CEO payroll and activate banking, accounting, insurance, contracting, and India employment/EOR support.
- Confirm the India Engineering Lead and named pool members, availability, loaded costs, and notice periods.

### Days 91–180: deliver and expand

- Complete and obtain acceptance for the Initial Delivery Milestone.

### Days 181–365: scale with control

- Reach $3.0M signed bookings and $2.5M recognized net revenue targets.
- Reach at least $1.5M of HSG/Agent Ramp-sourced signed SOW value.

## 15. Recommended negotiating position

The commercial package should be treated as one integrated exchange:

- HSG receives 60% for funding the agreed Year 1 runway and providing measurable commercial access.
- The Founding CEO receives 25% for accepting formation and execution risk and building the business, subject to three-year vesting.
- Employees receive access to a meaningful 15% pool to build the team.
- The CEO controls day-to-day operations and is accountable for the P&L.
- HSG and Agent Ramp interact with NewCo through arm's-length contracts, clear sales vehicles, and clean books.
- Delivery capacity is built from the India pool and scaled against signed work.
- The Canada CTO is useful but not a condition to launch; the India-led contingency is funded and operationally credible.

The recommended business starting point is therefore **a $2.0M committed Year 1 base plan, increased to $2.2M only if the Canada CTO joins**, together with the 60% / 25% / 15% capitalization and the objective six-month delivery cliff described above.
